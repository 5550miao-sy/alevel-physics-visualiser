What is actually moving?

金属中的自由电子持续做无规则运动。接通电源后，它们在无规则运动之上产生很小的整体漂移，从而形成电流。

Electron drift←→Conventional current

Reading the equation

n 是每立方米的载流子数量，A 是导线截面积，v 是平均漂移速度，q 是单个载流子的电荷。任何一项增大，电流都会增大。

COMMON MISCONCEPTION · 常见误区

电子漂移很慢，但灯泡几乎立即亮起。原因不是某个电子瞬间从开关跑到灯泡，而是电路中的电场迅速建立，使整条导线内的电子几乎同时开始漂移。

Amplitude

The maximum displacement of a point on the wave from its equilibrium position.

Wavelength

The shortest distance between two points that are in phase.

Frequency

The number of complete oscillations passing a point per unit time.

EXAM LANGUAGE

Particles oscillate about fixed equilibrium positions; they do not travel with the wave. The wave transfers energy without a net transfer of matter.

UNIT 2 · TOPIC 3A.2

Wave types

比较介质中粒子的振动方向与能量传播方向，区分 transverse waves 和 longitudinal waves。

Classification rule Compare the direction of oscillation with the direction of energy transfer.
Particle motion lab
切换波的类型，追踪橙色粒子如何围绕平衡位置振动
Wave typeTransverse
oscillation ⟂ energy transfer
Transverse wave

The oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer. Examples: electromagnetic waves and waves on a stretched string.

Longitudinal wave

The oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer. Examples: sound waves in air.

Compression and rarefaction

A compression is a region of higher particle density; a rarefaction is a region of lower particle density.

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

The drawn sinusoidal shape of a longitudinal wave is often a graph of displacement or pressure. It does not mean that particles move up and down.

Wave phase and superposition

调节两个同频波之间的 phase difference，观察每一点的 displacement 如何相加形成 resultant wave。

Principle of superposition When waves meet, the resultant displacement at any point is the vector sum of the individual displacements.
Superposition lab
蓝色 Wave 1 + 橙色 Wave 2 = 绿色 resultant
Phase difference φ0°
φ = 360° × Δx / λ
Constructive interference
Variables & units · φ = 360° × Δx/λ
φ phase difference, measured in degrees (°)
Δx path difference, measured in metres (m)
λ wavelength, measured in metres (m)
Δx and λ must use the same length unit.
In phase

Phase difference = 0° or a whole-number multiple of 360°. Crests align with crests.

Antiphase

Phase difference = 180°. A crest aligns with a trough; equal amplitudes cancel completely.

Path and phase difference

A path difference of λ gives 360°; λ/2 gives 180°; λ/4 gives 90°.

EXAM LANGUAGE

Superposition does not permanently change the individual waves. After overlapping, each wave continues with its original shape and direction.

Current–voltage relationships

拖动 I–V 图上的工作点，把宏观曲线与载流子、晶格振动、碰撞和半导体势垒直接联系起来。

How to read the graph Current is plotted on the y-axis and potential difference on the x-axis. The gradient ΔI/ΔV is the conductance, so a steeper gradient means a smaller resistance.
Variables & units · V = IR, R = V/I, P = IV
V potential difference, measured in volts (V)
I current, measured in amperes (A)
R resistance, measured in ohms (Ω)
P electrical power, measured in watts (W)
ΔI/ΔV gradient of this I–V graph, equal to conductance 1/R
I–V characteristicDrag the blue operating point
Metal latticeconstant temperature
Potential difference0.00 V
Current0.00 A
Resistance V/I—
Temperature293 K
Ohmic conductor · constant temperature

Increasing potential difference increases the electric field and mean drift velocity. At constant temperature the lattice vibration and resistance remain approximately constant, so current is directly proportional to potential difference.

Ohmic conductor

Straight line through the origin. At constant temperature, resistance is constant. The mean time between scattering events is approximately unchanged while drift velocity increases.

Filament lamp

Decreasing gradient at larger |V|. Heating makes the positive metal ions vibrate more, increasing electron–lattice collisions and resistance.

Semiconductor diode

Current mainly in one direction. Forward bias reduces the junction barrier; reverse bias widens the depletion region. The relevant carriers are electrons and holes—not free electrons colliding with metal ions.

NTC thermistor

Increasing gradient as self-heating increases. A hotter semiconductor has more mobile charge carriers, so resistance falls. Increased carrier density dominates over increased lattice scattering.

EXAM PRECISION

For an I–V graph, resistance is not the gradient: gradient = ΔI/ΔV = 1/R. For a V–I graph the gradient would be resistance. Always check which quantity is on each axis.

Resistivity

调节导电路径的长度与直径，观察几何形状和材料如何共同决定电阻。把宏观公式与电子可用的通道数量直接联系起来。

Material property Resistivity ρ describes how strongly a material opposes current. For a uniform sample at constant temperature, R = ρL/A.
Variables & units · R = ρL/A and A = πr²
R resistance, measured in ohms (Ω)
ρ resistivity, measured in ohm metres (Ω m)
L sample length, measured in metres (m)
A cross-sectional area, measured in square metres (m²)
r wire radius in metres; if diameter d is measured, r = d/2
π mathematical constant; no unit
Adjustable current path
Change the sample length and diameter; temperature is held constant
5.60 Ω
calculated resistance
Resistance R5.60 Ω
Resistivity ρ1.10 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m
Sample length L1.00 m

R = ρL/Aand for a circular wireA = πr²
Nichrome · long, narrow current path

Increasing length gives electrons a longer path through the lattice, so resistance increases in direct proportion to L. Increasing diameter creates a larger cross-sectional area—more parallel routes for charge—so resistance decreases in inverse proportion to A.

Longer sample

For the same material and area, R ∝ L. Doubling L doubles the resistance.

Wider sample

For the same material and length, R ∝ 1/A. Doubling the diameter makes A four times larger, so R becomes one quarter.

Different material

ρ depends on the material and temperature, not on the sample dimensions. Keep temperature constant when comparing values.

EXAM PRECISION

The micrometer measures diameter d. Convert d from mm to m, calculate r = d/2, then use A = πr². Cross-sectional area is in m², not m.

No emf: delocalised electrons still move rapidly and collide, but their directions are random. Over time there is no net movement of charge, so the current is zero.
Regular metal lattice

Positive metal ions remain at fixed mean positions in a regular lattice. They vibrate, but they do not drift through the wire.

Delocalised electrons

Outer electrons are not attached to one atom and can move throughout the metal, making them mobile charge carriers.

Electrical resistance

Collisions scatter electrons and transfer energy to the lattice, opposing their net drift through the conductor.

EXAM DRAWING

Show the electron path changing direction sharply at each collision. With an emf, add a small overall displacement towards the positive terminal; do not draw every electron travelling in a straight line.

UNIT 3 · PRACTICAL SKILLS IN PHYSICS I

Practical Hub

Unit 3 assesses how you plan, carry out, process and evaluate the eight Core Practicals from Units 1 and 2. This hub keeps the full practical treatment separate from the shorter theory lesson.

01

Aim, theory and variables

Start by turning the equation into a testable relationship.

Aim

Determine the electrical resistivity ρ of a uniform metal wire by measuring its cross-sectional area and the resistance of several measured lengths.

R = ρL/A   →   R = (ρ/A)L

A graph of R against L should be a straight line with gradient m = ρ/A. Therefore ρ = mA.

Why use a graph?

A single value ρ = RA/L uses only one reading and is strongly affected by random uncertainty. A best-fit line uses all readings, reveals anomalies and makes the relationship between R and L visible.

A positive y-intercept can indicate resistance from leads or contacts. It does not automatically invalidate the gradient.

INDEPENDENT VARIABLELength L / m

Change using a movable crocodile clip; measure from the fixed zero contact to the moving contact.

DEPENDENT VARIABLEResistance R / Ω

Measure V and I, then calculate R = V/I for each length.

CONTROL VARIABLESMaterial, area and temperature

Use the same uniform wire, keep its diameter unchanged and minimise heating by using a low current.

02

Apparatus and circuit

The voltmeter is parallel to the test length; the ammeter is in series.

low-voltage d.c. supplyswitchvariable resistorammetervoltmeteruniform wiremetre rulercrocodile clips

Micrometer measurement

  • Check and correct any zero error before use.
  • Use the ratchet so the wire is not compressed.
  • Take paired readings at right angles at several positions.
  • Calculate mean diameter d, then r = d/2 and A = πr².
03

Method and safety

A complete method explains what is measured, how it is changed and how errors are reduced.

  1. Check the micrometer for zero error. Measure diameter d at several positions along the wire and in two perpendicular directions at each position.
  2. Calculate the mean diameter. Convert it to metres, find radius r = d/2, then calculate A = πr².
  3. Fix the wire straight beside a metre ruler. The fixed contact should be at the zero mark; do not stretch the wire.
  4. Build the circuit shown. Put the ammeter in series and the voltmeter in parallel across only the measured length of wire. Start with high variable resistance.
  5. Choose a length L. Place the moving contact at the ruler mark and read L at eye level.
  6. Close the switch briefly. Record V and I once stable, then open the switch. Calculate R = V/I.
  7. Repeat and extend the range. Take repeat readings for at least six well-spaced lengths, identify anomalies and calculate mean R where appropriate.
  8. Plot and analyse. Plot R against L, draw a best-fit line, calculate its gradient using a large triangle, then use ρ = gradient × A.
Safety: a large current can heat the wire enough to cause burns. Use a low-voltage supply and large series resistance, close the switch only while taking readings, switch off between readings and do not touch the wire until it has cooled.

05

Uncertainty, errors and evaluation

Unit 3 expects a specific improvement linked to a specific problem.

Quantifying uncertainty

  • Use each instrument's actual resolution to assign absolute uncertainty to d, L, V and I.
  • Because A = πr², approximately ΔA/A = 2Δr/r = 2Δd/d.
  • From steepest and shallowest acceptable lines, Δm = (mmax − mmin)/2.
  • For ρ = mA, percentage uncertainties add: Δρ/ρ ≈ Δm/m + ΔA/A.
  • Compare experimental ρ with a data value using percentage difference, then judge whether the uncertainty ranges overlap.

Quality vocabulary

  • Precision: spread and resolution of repeated readings.
  • Accuracy: closeness to an accepted value; affected by systematic errors.
  • Reliability: consistency of repeated results and reproducibility.
  • Validity: whether the method actually tests R against L while other relevant factors remain controlled.
  • Anomaly: investigate and repeat; do not remove a point without justification.

06

Unit 3 exam prompts

Open each question to compare it with a mark-scheme style answer.

Why measure the diameter at several positions and at right angles?

The wire may not be perfectly circular or uniform. Repeated perpendicular measurements reduce random uncertainty and give a mean diameter that better represents the sample.

Why must the current be kept small?

Electrical heating raises the wire temperature. Since resistivity depends on temperature, heating changes the quantity being measured and makes the comparison between lengths invalid.

Why is a graph preferable to calculating ρ from one reading?

The best-fit gradient uses all readings, reduces the influence of random uncertainty, reveals anomalies and separates the relationship from a possible contact-resistance intercept.

What does a positive y-intercept on the R–L graph suggest?

There may be an additional approximately constant resistance from connecting leads, crocodile clips or contacts, or a zero offset in the measuring system.

How would you improve the percentage uncertainty in ρ?

Use a longer/wider range of L to reduce gradient uncertainty; repeat R readings; use a micrometer for d; repeat d at several positions and orientations; and use meters with suitable resolution. Because area depends on d², improving the diameter measurement is especially important.

How do you decide whether the result agrees with a data-book value?

Calculate percentage difference and compare it with the experimental percentage uncertainty. Agreement is supported if the accepted value lies within the experimental uncertainty range.
